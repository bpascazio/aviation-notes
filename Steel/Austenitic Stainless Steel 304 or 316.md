### Austenitic Stainless Steel (Typically Grade 304 or 316)

- **What it is:** A corrosion-resistant chromium-nickel alloy.
    
- **Where it’s used:** Brackets, tangs, and structural gussets used in airframes that combine aluminum framing tubes with steel hardware (such as the SkyReach BushCat, which utilizes stainless steel brackets on its aluminum tubular space frame).
    
- **Why it’s chosen:** When steel fittings are bolted directly against aluminum tubing (like 6061-T6), standard carbon steels can accelerate galvanic corrosion. Stainless steel provides high inherent corrosion resistance and a stable electrochemical pairing when isolated correctly from aluminum, making it ideal for exposed or semi-exposed external joints.
### What is "Austenitic" Stainless Steel?

**Austenitic** refers to the specific internal crystal structure (microstructure) of the steel, known as the _austenite phase_ (a face-centered cubic crystal lattice).

- **The Composition:** Standard carbon steel only has this crystal structure at very high temperatures. To keep that structure stable at room temperature, metallurgists add high amounts of alloying elements—specifically **chromium** (usually 16% to 26%) and **nickel** (usually 6% to 22%). This family of stainless steels is called _austenitic_ (common grades include 304 and 316).
    
- **Why it matters:**
    
    - **Corrosion Resistance:** The high chromium content reacts with oxygen to form a passive, self-healing chromium oxide layer on the surface, making it highly resistant to rust and chemical attack.
        
    - **Non-Magnetic:** Because of the face-centered cubic crystal arrangement, austenitic stainless steels are non-magnetic in their annealed state (though they can become slightly magnetic if heavily bent or cold-worked).
        
    - **Ductility and Toughness:** They can be easily formed, bent, and welded without losing their toughness, making them great for stamped brackets and fittings. However, they cannot be hardened by heat treatment—they only get harder when physically worked or bent (cold-working).
