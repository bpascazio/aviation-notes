**Strontium chromate primer** (often spelled _strontium chromate_) is a high-performance, anti-corrosive chemical primer heavily used in the aerospace industry. It is specifically engineered to protect lightweight metal structures—most notably aluminum alloys (like 2024 and 6061) and magnesium—from oxidation and filiform corrosion.

  

### What Makes It Special?

- **Active Corrosion Inhibition:** Unlike standard primers that merely act as a physical barrier to block moisture, strontium chromate contains active chemical inhibitors. If moisture or chlorides manage to scratch the paint and reach the metal, the chromate compounds chemically react to "heal" the spot and prevent rust or aluminum oxidation from spreading underneath the film.
    
      
    
- **Exceptional Adhesion:** It provides an elite bonding foundation between bare metal (or chemically treated metal) and subsequent polyurethane or epoxy topcoats.
    
      
    
- **The Color:** It is typically bright yellow or light green (often referred to in aviation circles by military specification shades like "zinc chromate yellow," though strontium chromate offers superior performance in high-humidity/marine environments compared to standard zinc chromate).
    
      
    

### How to Apply Strontium Chromate Primer

Applying aerospace-grade epoxy/polyurethane strontium chromate primers requires strict adherence to surface prep and safety protocols.

  

#### 1. Safety First (Crucial Step)

Strontium chromate contains **hexavalent chromium compounds**, which are known carcinogens and hazardous to health.

  

- Always wear a **respirator with organic vapor cartridges and particulate filters (P100)**, nitrile gloves, and a full paint suit or eye protection.
    
      
    
- Apply only in a well-ventilated area or a professional paint booth.
    
      
    

#### 2. Surface Preparation (The Foundation of Success)

Aluminum will reject paint if it is not chemically pristine.

  

- **Degrease:** Clean the metal thoroughly with a solvent (like isopropyl alcohol or acetone) to remove all oils, fingerprints, and shop grime.
    
      
    
- **Conversion Coating / Etching:** For maximum corrosion resistance, bare aluminum should be treated with a chemical conversion coating (such as Alodine / MIL-DTL-5541) or acid-etched. This turns the shiny metal a golden-iridescent or light green hue, creating microscopic roughness that the primer can bite into.
    
      
    
- **Scuffing (if required):** Follow the specific manufacturer's technical data sheet (TDS). Some primers require a light scuff with a maroon Scotch-Brite pad on aged primers or specialized surfaces.
    
      
    

#### 3. Mixing the Components

Most aviation strontium chromate primers are **multi-component systems** (typically a 2-part or 3-part mix: Base, Catalyst/Curing Agent, and sometimes a reducer/solvent).

  

- Stir the base component thoroughly to ensure settled pigments are fully integrated.
    
      
    
- Mix the base and catalyst precisely by volume or weight per the manufacturer's instructions (e.g., 1:1). Never guess ratios.
    
      
    
- **Induction Time:** Many epoxy-based chromate primers require an **induction time** (letting the mixed liquid sit for 15 to 30 minutes) before spraying so the chemical cross-linking can begin.
    
      
    
- Pay attention to the **pot life** (how long you have to spray it before it hardens in the pot, usually 4 to 6 hours).
    
      
    

#### 4. Application (Spray or Brush)

- **Equipment:** While small touch-ups can be brushed, these primers are typically applied using an HVLP (High Volume Low Pressure) spray gun for an even, ultra-thin film.
    
      
    
- **Film Thickness:** Aerospace primers are designed to be light. The target dry film thickness (DFT) is usually very thin—often between **0.3 to 0.8 mils** (approx. 8 to 20 microns). Putting it on too thick can lead to solvent entrapment or poor adhesion.
    
      
    
- **Technique:** Spray a smooth, wet tack coat, allow it to flash off briefly if specified, followed by a uniform cross-coat.
    
      
    

#### 5. Curing and Overcoating

- Allow the primer to flash and cure according to ambient temperature guidelines.
    
      
    
- Most epoxy strontium chromate primers have a **recoat window** (e.g., they must be topcoated with your polyurethane or epoxy finish coat within 24 to 48 hours, or else they must be lightly scuffed to achieve mechanical adhesion for the next layer).
