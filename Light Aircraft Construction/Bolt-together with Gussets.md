**Bolt-together aircraft with gussets** represent a popular structural construction method used primarily in experimental amateur-built (E-AB) aircraft, light-sport aircraft (LSA), and ultralights. Instead of using traditional welded steel tube frames or monocoque sheet-metal riveted shells, this technique uses structural metal tubes joined together by flat reinforcement plates—known as **gussets**—secured with aviation-grade bolts or structural rivets.

### How the Construction Works

1. **The Tube Framework:** The airframe skeleton (fuselage cages, cabin areas, or tail booms) is built using straight lengths of structural metal tubing—most commonly 6061-T6 aluminum square or round tubing.
    
2. **The Gusset Sandwich:** Where tubes intersect or butt together, matching flat metal plates (gussets) are placed on opposite sides of the joint, creating a sandwich configuration.
    
3. **Fastening:** Holes are precision-drilled through the gussets and the internal tubes. The assembly is then locked together using structural bolts (such as AN-hardware) or high-shear structural blind rivets.
    

### Mechanics and Load Distribution

In engineering terms, gusset plates are critical for **stress distribution**:

- **Eliminating Stress Concentration:** Rather than forcing all bending, shear, and tensile loads through a single point or a rigid weld boundary, a gusset spreads the load across a multi-fastener pattern.
    
- **Redundancy:** Using multiple bolts or rivets provides secondary load paths. If one fastener experiences unexpected stress, adjacent fasteners share the load, dramatically improving fatigue resistance.
    

### Materials Typically Used

- **Gusset Plates:** High-strength aluminum alloys like **2024-T3** or **7075-T6** are cut to precise geometric profiles to match specific joint angles.
    
- **Tubing:** Thin-walled aluminum tubing that provides an optimal stiffness-to-weight ratio while remaining easy to cut and fit in a home workshop.
    
- **Hardware:** AN-spec bolts (AN3 and AN4) paired with prevailing torque locknuts, or specialized structural rivets (such as Cherry or Pop rivets) depending on the designer's structural specifications.
    

### Advantages of the Method

- **No Welding Required:** Traditional welded chromoly steel frames require specialized TIG welding skills, precise jigs, and post-weld heat treatment checks. Bolt-together construction lowers the technical barrier to entry, allowing builders to assemble an airframe safely using basic workshop tools like band saws, drill presses, and deburring tools.
    
- **Inspectability:** Unlike internal welds or blind structural bonds, bolted joints are remarkably easy to inspect during condition inspections. Loose hardware, elongation of holes, or stress corrosion can be spotted immediately.
    
- **Modularity:** Damaged sections can often be unbolted and replaced individually without cutting or torching major structural elements.
    

### Critical Engineering Considerations

While accessible, this method requires strict adherence to plans regarding **edge distance** (how close a bolt hole is to the edge of the gusset), **hole tolerances** to prevent slop or fretting, and **torque limits** to avoid crushing thin-walled tubing. Proper priming and corrosion-prevention measures are also vital where different metal components meet.
