Instead of using closed, round aluminum tubes everywhere, channeled aluminum relies on **extruded metal profiles shaped like a "C" or "U" (channels)**.

![[Aluminum C Channel.png]]

- **How it’s used:** This was the signature construction method of early, ultra-lightweight aircraft like the classic Quicksilver series. Flat aluminum or steel gusset plates are sandwiched against the open faces or sides of these aluminum channels, and everything is tied together with through-bolts, rivets, and channel clips.
    
- **Why manufacturers used it:**
    
    - **Manufacturing Ease:** Aluminum is cheap to extrude into specific channel shapes. It eliminates complex welding or expensive precision-milled parts.
        
    - **Modularity:** It allows for quick bolt-together assembly, making kits easy for builders to assemble in a garage with basic hand tools.
        
- **Engineering Trade-offs:**
    
    - **Torsion (Twisting):** Open channels have lower resistance to twisting forces compared to closed round tubes. Because of this, channeled aluminum airframes historically relied heavily on external wire-bracing (flying and landing wires) to keep the wings and tail rigid.
        
    - **Crush Susceptibility:** If a mechanic or builder over-torques a bolt passing through a C-channel, the open flanges can pinch inward and distort unless internal spacer sleeves (bushings) are used.
