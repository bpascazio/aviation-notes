The **Bearhawk** is a highly regarded line of rugged, heavy-payload utility and backcountry aircraft. Known for their exceptional load-carrying capabilities, short takeoff and landing (STOL) performance, and impressive cross-country speeds, they have built a massive following among bush pilots and recreational flyers alike.

  

### Part 1: Are Bearhawk Aircraft Type-Certified?

**No, none of the aircraft in the Bearhawk line are FAA type-certified.**

  

- **Amateur-Built / Experimental Category:** Bearhawks are supplied as **kits or plans for amateur construction** (Homebuilt/Experimental category).
    
      
    
- **Design Philosophy:** Bob Barrows deliberately chose the experimental route to bypass the rigid, expensive paperwork and testing regimens of standard FAA type certification. This allows builders to modify components, integrate custom avionics, and experiment with different engine packages.
    
      
    
- **Note on Engines:** While the airframes themselves are experimental, individual component options—such as certain Lycoming engines or the type-certified DeltaHawk DHK180 diesel engine—can be type-certified items installed into the experimental airframe.
    
      
    

### Part 2: History of the Bearhawk

- **Origins (Mid-1990s):** The Bearhawk was originally designed and built by **Bob Barrows** in Virginia in the mid-1990s. Barrows wanted a personal utility aircraft that could reliably perform heavy backcountry hauling—something that could carry a pilot, multiple passengers, heavy gear, and even spare aircraft engines into rough, unpaved strips, while still maintaining respectable cruise speeds.
    
      
    
- **Evolution of Production:** Initially, Barrows sold raw plans allowing builders to source their own materials. As popularity exploded, production shifted to providing prefabricated kits and sub-assemblies (with manufacturing handled in Fairview, Oklahoma, and parts built in Mexico).
    
      
    
- **Design Hallmarks:** Across the entire line, Bearhawks share common structural DNA: a robust **4130 steel-tube fuselage** (gas-welded for stress normalization), a flush-riveted **all-aluminum wing** using advanced **Riblett airfoils** (for high speed yet docile stall characteristics), **oleo-strut landing gear** with oil dampening to absorb heavy bush impacts, and a unique tail "stinger" suspension.
    
      
    

### Part 3: The Complete Bearhawk Aircraft Line

The Bearhawk family has expanded from the original 4-seat utility machine into a diverse line ranging from light-sport trainers to 6-seat heavy haulers:

  

#### 1. Bearhawk Four / Bearhawk Bravo (4-Place)

- **The Flagship:** The original aircraft that started it all. It features a roomy 42.5-inch-wide cabin, a massive rear cargo area, and side-doors plus a wide utility cargo door.
    
      
    
- **Bearhawk Bravo:** Introduced as an evolution of the 4-place design, the Bravo features a revised wing with a greater span and area utilizing a modern Riblett airfoil, netting higher top speeds without sacrificing its low-speed backcountry manners.
    
      
    
- **Typical Power:** Lycoming O-360 up to O-540 (250–280 hp).
    
      
    

#### 2. Bearhawk Patrol (2-Place Tandem)

- **The Backrunner:** Introduced in 2002, the Patrol is a tandem (front-to-back) seating variant optimized specifically for sport flying and pure backcountry exploration.
    
      
    
- **Characteristics:** It has a narrower cabin (32 inches wide) and lighter gross weight, offering phenomenal visibility, nimble handling, and tight-spot STOL capabilities.
    
      
    
- **Typical Power:** Lycoming O-360/IO-390 variants (180–220 hp).
    
      
    

#### 3. Bearhawk Companion (2+2 Seating / Side-by-Side)

- **The Side-by-Side Cruiser:** Introduced to fill the gap for pilots preferring side-by-side seating, the Companion features a wide cabin layout.
    
      
    
- **Characteristics:** It can be configured as a strict two-seater with an enormous cargo bay behind the seats or fitted with a lightweight rear bench/jump-seat setup (hence "2+2"), blending utility with close-quarters flight instruction or cross-country comfort.
    
      
    
- **Typical Power:** 180–220 hp engines.
    
      
    

#### 4. Bearhawk LSA (Light Sport Aircraft)

- **The Light Alternative:** Designed to comply with Light Sport Aircraft weight limitations while retaining the signature Bearhawk strength.
    
      
    
- **Characteristics:** Tandem seating built lighter for pilots seeking lower operating costs, simple maintenance, and eligibility under sport pilot rules (though it can also be registered at a slightly higher gross weight in the experimental amateur-built category).
    
      
    
- **Typical Power:** Lower-horsepower engines (65 to ~147 hp), such as lighter four-stroke options.
    
      
    

#### 5. Bearhawk Five (6-Place)

- **The Heavy Hauler:** The largest aircraft in the family, built to accommodate up to six people or immense quantities of expedition and bush cargo.
    
      
    
- **Characteristics:** Features a stretched fuselage and higher gross weight capacity, making it a popular choice for bush-plane operators, remote outfitters, and families needing maximum payload flexibility.
    
      
    
- **Typical Power:** High-output engines ranging from 260 to 350 hp (often large-displacement Lycoming six-cylinders).
